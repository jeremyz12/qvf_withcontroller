# Batch 38 — WRITE-side extractor swap: does a stronger card builder raise the QVF ledger ceiling?

Hypothesis under test (from batch 36b): the ledger arm is reader-insensitive (smoc 91.4%@haiku vs 90.7%@sonnet-5) while plain full-context reading with sonnet-5 reaches 97.1%, so the ledger's ceiling is set by WRITE-side card extraction, not by the reader. Batch 38 rebuilds the store with extractor = claude-sonnet-5 (store results/wt_cards_v47s, 36 chains) and reruns the ledger arm with both readers on the same 140 questions.

Questions: results/b35_questions_sample36.jsonl (140 qids / 36 chains); corpus data/wikistate_full_ALL_v24.json (v2.4).
Runner: scripts/lb_reader_arm_b36b.py (unchanged from batch 36b).
Builder: scripts/wt_qvf_prototype_b38.py — see results/b38_provenance.txt for the exact diff vs the frozen scripts/wt_qvf_prototype.py.

## 4. Write-side diagnostic: compiled ledger vs gold chain

## 5. POST-HOC: slot-name fragmentation (NOT pre-registered)

Found while reading the 4 questions both v47s readers still get wrong. A chain is 'fragmented' in a store when the gold chain's values land under MORE THAN ONE card `slot` name — e.g. the gold slot `position` split into `parliament_membership` + `civic_office`. The ledger renderer prints `slot: value` per row, so a reader asked 'how many times did my position change' counts only one of the two lanes.

| chain | gold rows | v45 slot names | v47s slot names |
|---|---|---|---|
| wikiP39000-Q4976518 | 3 | political_position | committee_membership, occupation_role |
| wikiP39017-Q24568849 | 5 | position | civic_role, parliament_membership |
| wikiP39023-Q18527003 | 5 | political_position | parliament_membership, public_office |
| wikiP39033-Q5331705 | 6 | political_position | civic_appointment, parliament_membership |
| wikiP39037-Q3525068 | 4 | occupation | occupation, parliament_membership |
| wikiP39039-Q11801709 | 8 | parliament_membership, position | civic_office, parliament_membership |
| wikiP551000-Q19845625 | 3 | europe_trip_destinations, residence_country | residence_country, upcoming_trip |

Chains whose gold values are split across >1 card slot name: **v45 2/36, v47s 7/36** — the stronger extractor uses more semantically precise slot names and therefore fragments MORE.

  paired on the 114 single-slot questions: smoc_v47s@sonnet5 vs plainctx@sonnet5 — smoc-only-right=1, plainctx-only-right=2, McNemar exact p=1

  paired on the 26 fragmented questions: smoc_v47s@sonnet5 vs plainctx@sonnet5 — smoc-only-right=1, plainctx-only-right=7, McNemar exact p=0.07031

Confound check — fragmented chains are longer (mean 4.9 gold rows vs 3.4), BUT plainctx@sonnet5 scores 92.3% on exactly those 26 questions, and v45@sonnet5 scores 88.5%. So the subset is not intrinsically unanswerable; it is specifically hard for the v47s ledger.

**Causal evidence, not just correlation:** v47s@sonnet5 fails 8 of the 26 fragmented questions. Of those 8 failures, **7 were answered correctly by v45@sonnet5** (same reader, same question, same corpus — only the store changed) and 7 by plainctx@sonnet5. So these failures are specific to THIS store, not to the questions.

### The 4 questions BOTH v47s readers still get wrong

- `wikiP39033-Q5331705_v2cc` [change_count] gold=5 | v47s@haiku='3' | v47s@sonnet5='3 times' | v45@haiku=OK v45@sonnet5=OK | plainctx@sonnet5 RIGHT
- `wikiP39039-Q11801709_v2cc` [change_count] gold=7 | v47s@haiku='6' | v47s@sonnet5='6' | v45@haiku=OK v45@sonnet5=X | plainctx@sonnet5 RIGHT
- `wikiP54020-Q98594955_v2cb` [count_before] gold=6 | v47s@haiku='5' | v47s@sonnet5='5' | v45@haiku=X v45@sonnet5=OK | plainctx@sonnet5 RIGHT
- `wikiP551005-Q42324799_v2lt` [longest_tenure] gold='Amsterdam' | v47s@haiku='Buddenbrookhaus (held for 11 years, from 1835 to 1846)' | v47s@sonnet5='Buddenbrookhaus (tied with Amsterdam at 11 years each, ' | v45@haiku=OK v45@sonnet5=OK | plainctx@sonnet5 RIGHT

Note: `wikiP54020-Q98594955_v2cb` is NOT a v47s regression — v45@haiku gets it wrong too. Its gold date is `2024-00-00`, rendered in both ledgers as `2024`, so a reader cannot tell whether it falls strictly before 2024-06-29 and conservatively drops it. That is a gold-side date-precision problem shared by both stores.

### Second write-side regression: date granularity

The gold chain carries day-precision dates for some rows (e.g. `1857-03-01`); the card's `stated_date` may coarsen that to the year. Counted over the gold rows each store actually matched:

- **v45**: 2 / 122 matched gold rows lose day precision in the rendered ledger (1.6%)
- **v47s**: 24 / 133 matched gold rows lose day precision in the rendered ledger (18.0%)

This is what breaks `wikiP551005-Q42324799_v2lt`: v45 kept `1857-03-01`, v47s wrote `1857`, so Buddenbrookhaus (1835-1846) and Amsterdam (1846-1857) tie at 11 years and both readers pick the wrong one. Gold is Amsterdam, which wins only on the month.
