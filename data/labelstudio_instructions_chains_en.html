WikiState Chain Verification — Annotator Guide

Background. We built a benchmark where a persona's life events (employer, position, team, residence) are told to an AI assistant across many chat sessions. From each persona's chat log we extracted a state chain: the dated sequence of values for one slot (e.g., employer: Caltech 1985 → CERN 1989 → Cambridge 1995). Your job is to check, for each item, that the chain is a faithful summary of the raw log shown below it.

For every item, check four things:

  1. Anchor exists — each SOURCE SENTENCE really appears in the log (they are highlighted in yellow where found; if a row's sentence has no highlight anywhere, that is a red flag — search the log to confirm).
  2. Date matches — each row's DATE matches the date of the session containing its anchor (session dates are the brown headers). A date like 1995-00-00 means "1995, month/day unknown" — that is fine as long as the year matches.
  3. Value matches — each row's VALUE is what its anchor sentence actually says (not a different organization/city/team).
  4. Nothing missing — scan the log for any OTHER clear statement that the persona started/moved/joined something new for this slot that is NOT in the chain. Casual mentions, plans, and hypotheticals do not count — only clear declarations of an actual change.

Verdicts. Correct and complete = all four checks pass. Has errors = any check fails (say which row and what, in the notes). Unsure = genuinely ambiguous after checking (say why).

Rules. Work alone; do not discuss items with other annotators until everyone has finished. Use only the "Label All Tasks" button. Expect roughly 1.5–2 minutes per item.
